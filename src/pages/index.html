Agent-Ready Video (ARV)

Agent-Ready Video (ARV) is an open standard that makes video usable by AI agents. Follow it and crawlers can find your video, agents can call it, and any player can start it at the right second. The same moment, rights and credit travel with it at every step.

ARV does not replace the standards you already use. It binds thirteen of them into one manifest, one validator and one badge, and adds only the five things video needs that nobody else defines.

The five things ARV adds

1

Moments

A moment is a time range on a video, named by its range: arv:vid_8f2c#t=12400,31000. Anyone can recompute its id.

2

Per-moment rights

Each moment says what an agent may do with it: show it, quote up to N seconds, embed the source, and whether credit is required.

3

Evidence

Typed spans (transcript, on-screen text, shot caption, visual) that sit inside the moment and show why it answers the question.

4

Player-neutral playback

One descriptor that any player can use to start at the right second. Start time is the rule; the end is advisory.

5

Usage receipts

A signed record of which moment an agent served, to whom, under which rights policy, with the citation it showed.

Three conformance levels

L1

Crawl-readable

Manifest, catalog, JSON-LD, sitemap, llms.txt and a license file. A static site can reach L1 with JSON alone.

L2

Agent-callable

L1 plus an MCP endpoint with the origin profile, moments that resolve, and short-lived playback tokens. No stream URLs in discovery.

L3

Rights and ledger

L2 plus published keys, signed receipts, and rights that hold under negative tests.

One validator checks all three and prints pass or fail per standard. A green run earns the badge "ARV L1", "L2" or "L3" with a dated report. The checks.
